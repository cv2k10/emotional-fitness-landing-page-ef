E / FBefore you begin

Welcome

A starting point, not a score.

You already know how to push through. This guide is about something quieter: noticing what is happening inside you while you carry everything else. Set aside around thirty minutes, find somewhere you won't be interrupted, and move through the four parts at your own pace. You can also return to any part on its own.

Answer for the last few weeks

Not your best day or your worst one. Notice what has been typical lately, and let that be enough.

Curiosity over judgement

Whatever you find is information, not a verdict. Emotional fitness is a skill you build over time, like any other you have already mastered.

02 / Recognise your patterns

Five prompts for a clearer picture.

You don't need to answer all five at once. Choose the prompt that feels most useful today and write without looking for a perfect answer.

1. When does my energy change?

What tends to happen just before I feel stretched, distracted, or depleted?

2. What is my first response under pressure?

Do I speed up, withdraw, take over, seek reassurance, or something else?

3. Which conversations stay with me?

What might they reveal about a value, boundary, or need that matters to me?

4. Where do I make room for recovery?

What helps me transition out of work, even briefly, and how often do I allow it?

5. What would a thoughtful response look like?

In one current situation, what might I choose if I had a little more space?

03 / A simple daily practice

Pause. Notice. Choose.

Use this two-minute check-in at a natural transition in your day: before a meeting, after a difficult conversation, or as you close your laptop.

01

Pause

Let your attention settle. Feel your feet on the floor, or follow one steady breath.

02

Name

What feeling, sensation, or thought is most present? Ordinary words are enough.

03

Ask

What might this moment be asking of me? Is there a need or boundary here?

04

Choose

What is one response I can take on purpose, rather than on autopilot?

05

Reflect

Afterwards, note what you learned. Return to it; don't try to perfect it.
